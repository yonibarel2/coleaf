# CoLeaf – Marketing

Oct 18, 2026

## Launch idea

Post a short poll on social platforms: "How do you write your repo .md files?" Most answers are the usual tools; one answer is CoLeaf. A link to the CoLeaf site sits right under the poll.

The poll does two jobs at once: it gets attention for CoLeaf, and the votes show how people write Markdown today.

## Draft poll

Four options, each under 25 characters, so it fits X and LinkedIn polls.

**Question:** How do you write your repo .md files?

1. VS Code / my IDE
2. Notion or Google Docs
3. Obsidian / Typora
4. Word-style in CoLeaf

**Post text:**

> Most teams keep their docs in the repo, but only developers enjoy editing them. I'm building CoLeaf: Word-style editing of your repo's .md files, with simple Git sync. Curious how you do it today.
>
> Try it: \[SITE LINK\]

## Where to post

Post the poll where you post as yourself (LinkedIn, X). In communities, lead with the question and keep the promotion light. Check each community's rules before posting.

| Platform | Built-in poll | Fit | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn | Yes | High: PMs, architects, team leads | Post from your own profile |
| X | Yes | Medium: developers | Short text; add a GIF of the editor |
| Reddit (r/git, r/Markdown, r/ObsidianMD) | Yes, in some subreddits | Medium | Many subreddits ban self-promotion; read the rules first |
| Hacker News | No | High once the app is live | Use "Show HN" with the product, not a poll |
| Discord / Slack groups | Varies | Medium | Ask the admin first |

## Verdict

Good idea, as long as it's open about being promotion. A poll is cheap, gets people involved, and gives you real data.

- **Risk:** if it looks like a fake survey, people ignore or report it. **Fix:** say in the post that you're building CoLeaf.
- **Risk:** CoLeaf gets few votes because nobody knows it yet. **Fix:** the goal is clicks on the link, not votes.
- **Timing:** post only once the site and a download or waitlist are live, so the link has somewhere to send people.
- **Bonus:** the results ("62% of you edit .md files in VS Code") make a good follow-up post.
